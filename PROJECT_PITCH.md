# Making residency work better: three feasible chief resident innovation pilots

## Vision

Residency training is challenging by design, but administrative friction, uneven teaching opportunities and unclear expectations do not have to be part of it. I propose a set of small, measurable digital improvements that free up time for learning and enhance communication between residents, faculty and program administration.

## Pilot 1 — Call Exchange

**Problem:** Finding someone willing and eligible to exchange call can involve multiple messages, unclear schedules and delays.

**Solution:** Upload existing CSV/Excel schedules, identify possible one-to-one swaps under clear rules, and route proposals through scheduler approval. The demo shows transparent matching and explicit limits; it does **not** actually change shifts.

**Initial pilot:** One small call pool with explicit scheduler policies and final manual approval. Compare time-to-approved-trade against the current process and review coverage fairness.

## Pilot 2 — Learning Map

**Problem:** Rotation teaching can depend on what happens to arise, rather than each trainee's identified developmental needs.

**Solution:** A self-directed check-in where residents or students choose topic areas, rate current confidence and select teaching priorities, with curated references to current standards, guidelines and textbook topics.

**Initial pilot:** One rotation with brief pre-rotation goal selection and a midpoint check-in. Assess whether requested teaching occurred and whether residents perceive greater ownership of learning. Institutional educators would first approve the competency mapping and selected references.

## Pilot 3 — Rotation Agreement

**Problem:** Expectations around supervision, call, autonomy, feedback and assessment are often implicit or vary from service to service.

**Solution:** A simple conversation-based agreement that the resident and supervisor review at the beginning of a rotation and acknowledge. A revised agreement prompts renewed acknowledgment.

**Initial pilot:** Use a short, locally approved agreement at one service, with standardized domains and optional fields. Measure early goal alignment and usefulness of midpoint feedback.

## Implementation approach

1. **Listen:** short interviews with residents, faculty, schedulers and program leadership to refine the top pain points.
2. **Co-design:** clarify existing policies, training-stage requirements, data access and approval boundaries.
3. **Pilot safely:** trial within a small consenting group, with manual oversight and no patient data.
4. **Measure and iterate:** track workflow time, user experience, teaching uptake and safety/equity concerns.
5. **Scale only after review:** validate privacy, security, feasibility, governance and maintenance ownership.

This public website is intentionally a **demonstration of possibilities**, not a completed institutional application. Its goal is to make the concepts tangible enough to invite collaboration and practical feedback.
